## Twenty-Four Rolls for Twelve Guests

Twelve guests at Thanksgiving need twenty-four dinner rolls. Two apiece.

That is one more per person than the 1.5 rolls the [holiday meal planner](/holiday-meal-planner/) prints in its sides list, and it is the difference between a basket that survives the second pass and one that is empty before the cranberry sauce gets to the end of the table.

The arithmetic is not the hard part. A roll is one ounce at one store and three at another, so a count without a weight is a guess.

## Two Rolls Is Two Ounces of Bread

The target is two ounces of bread per person. That covers the roll eaten with the meal and the one torn in half to mop up the last of the gravy.

So the answer moves with the size of the roll:

| Roll | Weight | Per person | Buy for 12 guests |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brown-and-serve, supermarket | 1 oz | 2 | 24 |
| Bakery dinner roll | 1½ oz | 1½ | 18 |
| Seeded kaiser or big deli roll | 2½ to 3 oz | 1 | 14 |

If your rolls are the big ones, twelve of them feed twelve people. If they are the little ones from the freezer case, the same table needs two dozen. Counting rolls is the wrong unit until you know the weight.

## How Many Rolls Per Person, by Headcount

The right-hand column assumes two-ounce rolls baked touching, which is what makes them pull apart instead of rolling off the plate.

| Guests | At 1.5 each | Buy at 2 each | Dozens | 9x13 pans |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 9 | 12 | 1 | 1 |
| 8 | 12 | 16 | 1½ | 1 |
| 10 | 15 | 20 | 2 | 1 |
| 12 | 18 | 24 | 2 | 1 |
| 16 | 24 | 32 | 3 | 2 |
| 20 | 30 | 40 | 3½ | 2 |
| 24 | 36 | 48 | 4 | 2 |
| 30 | 45 | 60 | 5 | 3 |

A 40-roll calculation is a 42-roll purchase, which is why the dozens column rounds up instead of matching the count. Running out is the part people remember.

## Stuffing Is Also Bread

A table with dressing has two bread dishes competing for the same appetite.

If your crowd takes a real scoop of stuffing, three-quarters of a cup is the number in [our stuffing math](/blog/stuffing-per-person-thanksgiving/), drop the rolls by a quarter and put the difference into a second pan of something people finish. If your family does turkey, potatoes, and rolls and calls that dinner, the full two per person is honest.

## Brown-and-Serve, Frozen Dough, or Scratch

Three ways to put hot bread on the table, and the choice is about the oven and the clock rather than virtue.

| Type | Lead time | Oven | Where it wins |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brown-and-serve | None, straight from the bag | 350°F, 8 to 12 min | The oven is already full and the clock is tight |
| Frozen dough balls | 3 to 5 hours on the counter, or overnight in the fridge | 350°F, 15 to 20 min | You want the smell without the mixing |
| Scratch | 2½ to 3 hours, two rises | 375°F, 15 to 18 min | You have a stand mixer and a free morning |

Brown-and-serve rolls are partially baked at the plant, so they need browning and nothing else. No rise, no bench time, no flour on the counter.

Frozen dough balls need a rise, three to five hours on a buttered sheet at room temperature according to the package. Overnight in the refrigerator is the better version, because a cold rise gives you a window instead of a deadline. Take the pan out an hour before baking so the dough loses its chill.

Scratch rolls are 375°F for 15 to 18 minutes. A 55 to 60 gram dough ball bakes to about two ounces, so weigh them if there is a scale. The ball that comes in at 48 grams bakes smaller and browner than its neighbors.

## Twenty-Four Rolls Fit One 9x13, Barely

A 9-by-13 pan laid out six by four holds 24 two-inch rolls with the edges just touching. That is the pull-apart arrangement, and it is what you want on a holiday table because the sides stay soft.

The same 24 rolls with an inch of space between them need a 17-by-11 footprint, which is an 18-by-13 half sheet, not a 9x13. Same dough, different crust: spaced rolls brown on the sides, touching ones tear into soft squares. The [baking pan converter](/baking-pan-converter/) covers the switch when your pan is a 10-by-15 or a 7-by-11.

Past 24 rolls you are into a second pan. A 32-roll table gets one full 9x13 and eight rolls on a smaller pan, and that second pan bakes faster, so it goes in five minutes later.

## The Oven Is at 325°F and the Rolls Want 375°F

The bird comes out to rest for 30 minutes. The rolls want a hotter oven than the turkey ever saw.

A home oven climbing from 325°F to 375°F takes eight to ten minutes. That leaves about 20 minutes of the rest for the bake, comfortable for brown-and-serve and just enough for scratch rolls at 18 minutes. The full sequence lives in our [Thanksgiving oven schedule](/blog/thanksgiving-oven-schedule/), and the tightest version puts the rolls in the moment the turkey comes out, before the foil goes on.

Check the underside of the center roll rather than the top when you are deciding. Tops brown first and lie.

## Butter, Salt, and Nothing Else

One tablespoon of melted butter brushes a dozen rolls. Two tablespoons covers 24, which is a quarter stick, and it goes on within a minute of the pan leaving the oven, while the crust is still soft enough to take it.

Half a teaspoon of flaky salt over each dozen is the one addition worth making. It does more for a supermarket roll than any herb butter.

Skip the brush if the package says the rolls are already glazed. They come out greasy and tasting of nothing but the butter you added.

## Teenagers and the Second Pan

Count anyone over 12 as two rolls, teenagers and anyone under 25 as three, and one and a half per child. The teenagers eat one standing at the counter before dinner and two at the table.

That is where the planner's 1.5 comes from: a plate number, adequate for adults behaving themselves. A table with teenagers needs a pan bigger than the arithmetic.

## Leftovers Freeze Better Than They Reheat

Baked rolls go stale within a day and freeze for two months. A zip bag, the air pressed out, then 350°F for five minutes in foil brings them back.

The reheat is where they fail. A roll microwaved for 30 seconds turns to rubber inside a minute and there is no fixing it afterward. Foil and a real oven, or split and toasted in a skillet with butter, which is the better use anyway: day-after turkey sandwich, cut side down.

## FAQ

### How many dozen rolls for 20 guests?

Forty rolls, so three and a half dozen, and buy four. Twenty guests at two each is 40, and the extra eight cover the guest who takes a roll, changes their mind about stuffing, and leaves the roll on the plate. Two 9x13 pans hold 48.

### Can I bake dinner rolls the day before Thanksgiving?

Yes. Bake them Wednesday, cool them completely uncovered so the crust does not steam soft, then bag them at room temperature. Thursday, reheat at 350°F for five to seven minutes in foil. Frozen dough balls make the day-before easier: shape and pan them Wednesday night, refrigerate covered, and bake straight into Thursday.

### How many rolls for 50 people?

One hundred, which is nine dozen. That is four 9x13 pans plus a half sheet, baked in relays of two pans at 18 minutes each, with the finished ones held in a cooler with the lid cracked so they stay crisp.
