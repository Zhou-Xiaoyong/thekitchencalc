## What a Cups-to-Grams Calculator Actually Does

A **convert cups to grams calculator** is a density lookup with a multiplication step. Cups measure volume; grams measure mass. To get from one to the other you need to know how heavy a cup of that specific ingredient is — and that number is different for flour, sugar, butter, and cocoa powder.

The formula is one line:

```
grams = cups × grams per cup (for that ingredient)
```

So 2 cups of all-purpose flour is 2 × 120 = **240 g**, and ½ cup of butter is 0.5 × 227 = **113.5 g**. Reverse it when a recipe gives grams and you only have cups:

```
cups = grams ÷ grams per cup
```

That is the entire math. The hard part is not the arithmetic — it is knowing the density, and remembering that "1 cup = 240 g" is only true for water.

## When Volume Is Fine — and When It Isn't

You do not need a scale for everything. Volume measuring is perfectly acceptable for:

- **Water, milk, stock, and most thin liquids** (density is close enough to 1 g/ml).
- **Small amounts of leaveners and spices**, where a teaspoon is more practical than 4 g.
- **Approximate dishes** — soups, stews, braises, stir-fries — where you adjust to taste anyway.

Weight measuring earns its keep when:

- **Baking**, where the flour-to-liquid ratio decides structure. A 30% flour error is the difference between tender and tough.
- **Scaling a recipe**, because multiplying 1⅔ cups is painful and error-prone while multiplying 267 g is trivial.
- **Working from international recipes**, since metric recipes list grams and converting them to cups introduces rounding at every step.
- **Sticky ingredients** — honey, peanut butter, molasses — where what clings to the cup never makes it into the bowl.

## Worked Example: Converting a Cookie Recipe

A recipe serves 24 and calls for:

- 2¼ cups all-purpose flour
- 1 cup butter, softened
- ¾ cup granulated sugar
- ¾ cup brown sugar, packed
- 2 cups chocolate chips

## Three Mistakes That Ruin Conversions

1. **Using the wrong cup.** U.S. 240 ml, metric 250 ml, and old UK 284 ml cups produce materially different weights. Check the recipe's origin.
2. **Packing ingredients that should not be packed.** Only brown sugar gets pressed in. Flour, cocoa, and powdered sugar are spooned and leveled.
3. **Assuming one density fits a category.** "Flour" ranges from 96 g/cup (almond) to 167 g/cup (semolina). "Sugar" ranges from 100 g (sifted powdered) to 220 g (packed brown). Always look up the specific ingredient.

A fourth, quieter mistake: converting each ingredient, scaling, then converting back, over and over. Convert to grams **once**, do all your scaling in grams, and only translate back to cups at the very end if you have no scale.
