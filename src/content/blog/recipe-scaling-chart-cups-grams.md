## Why "Cups to Grams" Matters When You Scale

Volume measurements are friendly until you start multiplying them. A recipe that calls for "1 cup flour" is easy to scoop, but when you scale it to feed a crowd the math gets messy: 2.5 cups, 3.75 cups, 6.25 cups. Worse, a "cup" of flour and a "cup" of honey weigh completely differently, so you can't just multiply volumes and expect consistent results.

That's why a reliable **recipe scaling chart cups to grams** is the first tool serious home bakers reach for. Grams are weight, not volume — they don't care how densely you scoop, and they scale by clean multiplication. Below is a chart you can keep bookmarked, followed by a free calculator that does the converting and the resizing for you at the same time.

## The Recipe Scaling Chart: Cups to Grams

These are the grams in a lightly spooned-and-leveled U.S. cup (240 ml). "Packed" ingredients are pressed into the cup; everything else is filled without shaking down.

| Ingredient | 1 cup (g) | ½ cup (g) | ⅓ cup (g) | ¼ cup (g) |
|------------|-----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| All-purpose flour | 120 | 60 | 40 | 30 |
| Bread flour | 130 | 65 | 43 | 33 |
| Granulated sugar | 200 | 100 | 67 | 50 |
| Brown sugar (packed) | 220 | 110 | 73 | 55 |
| Powdered sugar | 120 | 60 | 40 | 30 |
| Butter | 227 | 113 | 76 | 57 |
| Milk | 240 | 120 | 80 | 60 |
| Water | 237 | 118 | 79 | 59 |
| Honey / maple syrup | 340 | 170 | 113 | 85 |
| Vegetable oil | 218 | 109 | 73 | 55 |
| Cocoa powder | 85 | 43 | 28 | 21 |
| Rolled oats | 90 | 45 | 30 | 23 |
| Shredded cheese | 110 | 55 | 37 | 28 |

A few notes that save recipes:

- **Flour is the big one.** Scooping straight from the bag can pack in 30% more than spooning-and-leveling, which is why gram weights beat cups for repeatable baking.
- **Brown sugar must be packed.** If your cup is loose, you're short on sugar and your texture will suffer.
- **Liquids vary.** Honey is far heavier than water per cup, so never assume one liquid chart fits another.

## How to Use the Chart While Scaling

Once you've converted each ingredient to grams, scaling is just multiplication. To triple a cookie recipe, multiply every gram value by 3. To scale from 8 servings to 50, divide 50 by 8 (6.25) and multiply every gram by 6.25. No fractions, no "how many thirds of a cup is this."

## A Worked Scaling Example

Take a chocolate chip cookie recipe for 24 cookies:

- 2¼ cups flour (270 g), 1 cup butter (227 g), ¾ cup granulated sugar (150 g), ¾ cup brown sugar (165 g), 2 eggs, 2 tsp vanilla (8 g), 1 tsp salt (6 g), 2 cups chips (340 g).

You need 60 cookies — a factor of 2.5:

- Flour 675 g, butter 568 g, granulated sugar 375 g, brown sugar 413 g, 5 eggs, vanilla 20 g, salt 15 g, chips 850 g.

Now read those gram numbers back as cups if you prefer: about 5⅔ cups flour, 2½ sticks butter, and so on. The gram math is exact; the cup translation is just for the scoopers.
