## How Many Grams in a Cup of Sugar?

For standard U.S. **granulated sugar, 1 cup weighs 200 grams**. That is the number behind most American recipes, and it is the one to memorize.

The catch is that "sugar" covers a family of ingredients with very different densities. Brown sugar is heavier per cup because it is packed, powdered sugar is much lighter because it is full of air, and coconut sugar sits somewhere in between. Using 200 g for all of them will quietly wreck the texture of whatever you are baking.

| Sugar type | 1 cup (g) | ½ cup (g) | ⅓ cup (g) | ¼ cup (g) | 1 tbsp (g) |
|------------|-----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Granulated (white) sugar | 200 | 100 | 67 | 50 | 12.5 |
| Caster / superfine sugar | 200 | 100 | 67 | 50 | 12.5 |
| Brown sugar, packed | 220 | 110 | 73 | 55 | 13.8 |
| Brown sugar, loose | 180 | 90 | 60 | 45 | 11.3 |
| Powdered / confectioners' | 120 | 60 | 40 | 30 | 7.5 |
| Powdered, sifted | 100 | 50 | 33 | 25 | 6.3 |
| Coconut sugar | 160 | 80 | 53 | 40 | 10 |
| Raw / turbinado sugar | 200 | 100 | 67 | 50 | 12.5 |
| Maple sugar | 150 | 75 | 50 | 38 | 9.4 |
| Honey (liquid) | 340 | 170 | 113 | 85 | 21 |
| Maple syrup (liquid) | 322 | 161 | 107 | 80 | 20 |

Note the two liquid sweeteners at the bottom: they are measured by weight too, but a cup of honey weighs 70% more than a cup of granulated sugar. Never swap one for the other cup-for-cup.

## Why Brown Sugar Is Always "Packed"

Recipes that call for brown sugar almost always mean **firmly packed** — pressed into the dry measuring cup until it holds the cup's shape when turned out. That is why a packed cup weighs 220 g while a loosely scooped cup weighs about 180 g. The difference is roughly two tablespoons of sugar, plus the molasses that comes with it, which changes both sweetness and moisture.

Practical rules:

- If a recipe says "1 cup brown sugar" with no qualifier, pack it.
- If it says "lightly packed," press gently — you will land near 200 g.
- If your brown sugar has hardened, soften it (a slice of bread or a damp paper towel in the bag overnight) before packing. Hard lumps leave air pockets and you will under-measure.

## The Cup-Size Trap: US vs Metric vs UK

Sugar conversions usually assume the **U.S. legal cup of 240 ml**, which is what the 200 g figure is based on. But cups are not universal:

- **Metric cup (Australia, New Zealand, Canada in some recipes):** 250 ml → about **220 g** of granulated sugar.
- **Old UK/imperial cup:** 284 ml → roughly **250 g** of granulated sugar.
- **Japanese cup:** 200 ml → about **176 g** of granulated sugar.

That is a spread of more than 70 grams on the same recipe line, which is plenty to make caramel seize or a cake sink. If you are working from a non-U.S. recipe, check which cup the author means before converting — or better, find a version of the recipe that lists grams.

## Scaling Sugar by Weight

Sugar is the easiest ingredient to scale once it is in grams. Say a brownie recipe calls for 1¼ cups granulated sugar:

1. Convert: 1.25 × 200 g = **250 g**.
2. Scale to a 9×13 pan from an 8×8 (about 1.8×): 250 × 1.8 = **450 g**.
3. Read back if you want: 450 ÷ 200 = **2¼ cups**.

The gram path stays exact; the cup path accumulates rounding at every step. ## Sugar, Pan Size, and Bake Time

Sugar does more than sweeten: it tenderizes, browns, and holds moisture. Change the sugar-to-flour ratio and you change how the bake behaves. ## Quick Answers to Common Questions

- **Is 200 g of sugar the same as 1 cup?** For granulated sugar, yes (U.S. cup). Not for brown, powdered, or coconut sugar.
- **How do I measure 100 g without a scale?** Half a U.S. cup of granulated sugar is 100 g. For brown sugar, a scant ½ cup packed.
- **Can I reduce the sugar?** Usually by 10–25% in cookies, quick breads, and muffins without structural damage. Much beyond that and you lose browning, moisture, and spread.
- **Does powdered sugar need sifting before measuring?** Sift first, then measure — a sifted cup is about 100 g versus 120 g unsifted.
