## A 15-Pound Bird Goes In at 12:45

Dinner at 4:00, a 15-pound turkey, and the oven needs to be shut by 12:45. Thirteen minutes a pound is 195 minutes, which is 3 hours 15. Add 30 minutes of resting and carving and the platter lands on the table on time.

That 13-minute figure is where most Thanksgiving plans begin, and it is a good number for a big bird. It is wrong for a small one by the better part of an hour, which is how a 12-pound turkey ends up back in the oven at 3:45 while everyone holds a plate.

## What the Numbers Actually Look Like

These are the USDA roasting times at 325°F, unstuffed, bird starting from a 40°F fridge. Divide the hours by the pounds and you get the last column, which is where the 13-minute rule comes from.

| Bird | Roast time at 325°F | Minutes per lb | In the oven by (4:00 dinner) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 lb | 2¾ to 3 hr | 17 to 18 | 12:30 pm |
| 13 lb | 3 to 3¾ hr | 14 to 17 | 11:45 am |
| 16 lb | 3¾ to 4¼ hr | 14 to 16 | 11:15 am |
| 19 lb | 4¼ to 4½ hr | 13 to 14 | 11:00 am |
| 22 lb | 4½ to 5 hr | 12 to 14 | 10:30 am |

Read the middle rows and the shorthand makes sense. Thirteen or fourteen minutes a pound is correct across the 16 to 24-pound range, which is what most families buy.

Read the top row and it falls apart. Ten pounds at 13 minutes is 130 minutes, or 2 hours 10. USDA puts that bird at 2¾ hours minimum. You are short by 55 minutes before the oven door even closes.

## Why the Number Shrinks as the Bird Gets Heavier

Heat enters a turkey through its skin, and the distance from skin to the center of the thigh sets the schedule. Double the weight and the volume doubles, but the surface area grows by only about 60 percent. There is simply more meat behind each square inch of browning skin.

A 22-pound bird measures roughly five inches from the surface to the middle of the thigh. A 10-pounder is closer to three and a half. Two extra inches of meat add time, just not time that scales with weight, which is why minutes per pound drift downward as the bird grows.

The practical version: the lighter the bird, the more minutes each pound needs. Under 12 pounds, plan 17. From 12 to 15, plan 15. From 16 up, 13 holds.

## Decide the Bird Before the Clock

Sizing comes first, because the clock is a function of it. The standard allowance is 1 pound per person for a bone-in bird with modest leftovers, and 1.25 pounds if you want Thursday's sandwiches and a pot of Friday soup. Our [Holiday Meal Planner](/holiday-meal-planner/) runs that multiplier against your adult and child count, then converts the weight into thaw days and a roast estimate.

| Guests | Turkey at 1.25 lb each | Cook time at 325°F |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | 10 lb | 2¾ to 3 hr |
| 10 | 12.5 lb, buy a 12 to 14 | 3 to 3¾ hr |
| 12 | 15 lb | 3¾ to 4¼ hr |
| 16 | 20 lb | 4¼ to 4½ hr |
| 20 | 25 lb, or two 12-lb birds | 3 to 3¾ hr each |

That last row is the one that catches hosts. A 25-pound bird barely exists in a supermarket case, and a 30-inch home oven will not fit one in a pan with any room around it. Two 12-pounders roast on one rack, and USDA's own guidance is to set the timer by the smaller bird and check both.

Oven space is a separate arithmetic problem. A 20-pound bird in a 16x13 pan fills the rack, so the green bean casserole and the rolls need the last hour somewhere else. Count the sides that need 350°F before you commit to one giant bird.

## Six Things That Move the Clock

Every item below is a variable USDA names as a reason roasting times vary.

| Change | Effect on time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Stuffed instead of unstuffed | Add 20 to 30 min | Stuffing must also hit 165°F, and it insulates the cavity |
| Started still frozen | Add 50% or more | Giblets come out with tongs after 30 min in the oven |
| Convection at 325°F | Cut 25% | Moving air cooks faster, so a 16-lb bird lands near 3 hr 10 min |
| Foil tent left on the whole time | Add 20 to 30 min | Steam stays put, skin never browns |
| Pan deeper than 2½ inches | Add 15 to 25 min | Heat cannot circulate under the bird |
| Oven actually 25°F low | Shifts everything | A $8 oven thermometer is cheaper than a dry turkey |

The convection row comes from the same 0.75 factor our [Cooking Time Calculator](/cooking-time-calculator/) applies when you convert an oven time to a convection oven. If your fan oven runs hot, drop the dial to 300°F and keep the 325°F time.

## The Backward Timeline for a 4:00 Dinner

Work backward from the plate, not forward from the oven.

- **Four to five days out.** A 20-pound bird moves to the fridge, breast up on a tray, still in its wrapper. Thawing runs 24 hours for every 4 to 5 pounds, and the planner rounds that to 4 days for a 20-pounder.
- **One day out.** Brine now if you are brining. A whole turkey wants 12 to 24 hours in a 5% salt solution, which the [Brine Calculator](/brine-calculator/) sizes to the bird and the bucket.
- **12:30 pm.** Rack in the lower third, oven to 325°F, no preheat needed.
- **12:45 pm.** Bird in, breast up on a flat rack, half a cup of water in the pan.
- **2:15 pm.** Rotate the pan and tent the breast loosely if it is already dark.
- **3:15 pm.** First real temperature check in the thigh.
- **3:30 pm.** Out of the oven. Rest 20 minutes, loosely tented.
- **3:50 pm.** Carve.

## 165°F Decides, Not the Clock

The thigh at its innermost point, the wing, and the thickest part of the breast all need to read 165°F. Check all three. The thickest part of the breast often runs 10 degrees behind the thigh on a large bird.

Two details worth knowing. First, the temperature climbs another 5 to 10 degrees while the bird rests, so pulling it at 165°F means it finishes closer to 172°F. Second, the plastic pop-up timer that ships with many frozen birds is set to fire near 180°F in the breast, roughly 15 degrees past where the dark meat wants to be. It is a backstop, not a plan.

The full list of safe temperatures, including stuffing and leftovers, is on our [food safety temperature guide](/food-safety-temperature-guide/). Leftover turkey keeps 3 to 4 days in the fridge and needs reheating to 165°F, not just warming.

## FAQ

### How long does a 20-pound turkey take to cook?

Four and a half to five hours unstuffed at 325°F, and 4¾ to 5¼ hours stuffed. For a 4:00 dinner that means the bird goes in at 10:30 am. Thawing is the longer job: 4 to 5 days in the fridge, so it needs to move down from the freezer about a week ahead.

### Can I roast it at 350°F to finish faster?

Yes, and it saves roughly 15 to 20 percent of the time. A 15-pounder at 350°F lands near 3 hours instead of 3¾. Two costs come with it. The skin browns well before the thigh reaches 165°F, so tent the breast at the 90-minute mark. And the gap between white and dark meat widens, which is why 325°F remains the tested baseline.

### How many days ahead do I need to thaw it?

Allow 24 hours per 4 to 5 pounds in the refrigerator: about 3 days for a 12-pounder, 4 to 5 for a 20-pounder, 5 for a 24-pounder. If you are behind, a cold water bath runs about 30 minutes per pound with the water changed every half hour, and the bird must be cooked immediately afterward.
