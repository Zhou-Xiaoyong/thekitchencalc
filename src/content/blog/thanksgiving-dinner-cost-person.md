## Eighty-Four Dollars for Twelve

A 15-pound turkey, five pounds of potatoes, a pound of butter, two pies, and eight cans and boxes came to $83.97 at 2026 supermarket prices. Twelve people eat that for $7.00 a head, with leftovers for Friday.

The supermarket's heat-and-serve package for the same table costs $13 to $18 a person. A restaurant prix fixe starts near $45. Same turkey, same mashed potatoes, three different bills.

## Cost Per Edible Pound Is Double the Sticker

Turkey at $1.49 a pound is not turkey at $1.49 a pound. A bone-in bird yields about 50% carvable meat, so the 15-pounder puts 7½ pounds on the plate and the real price is $2.98 per edible pound. The [holiday meal planner](/holiday-meal-planner/) does this math per guest before you shop, which matters more than the shelf tag.

The same 50% rule explains why a boneless breast roast at $5.49 a pound is often the cheaper white meat. At 70% yield it lands at $7.84 per edible pound against $5.96 for bone-in breast at 55%. Per pound of meat served, the bone still wins on price and loses on gravy.

## Where the Bill Actually Goes

The bird is 27% of the total. Everything roasted around it is the rest.

Butter is the quiet line item. One pound covers the mash, the stuffing, and two pie crusts. At $4.99 a pound it costs more than the five pounds of potatoes it goes into.

Cream and eggs carry the desserts. Two pumpkin pies need two cans of pumpkin, two crusts, two cans of evaporated milk, and three eggs, which is $17.24 before whipped cream. That is $8.62 a pie against $16 to $22 from a bakery, and the bakery is charging for the crust rolling and an oven slot, not better pumpkin.

Canned and boxed components outsell fresh ones in the last week for a reason: they do not spoil if plans change. Three cans of green beans at $4.47 also undercut two pounds of fresh beans at $2.99 a pound.

## Three Routes to the Same Table of Twelve

| Route | Total for 12 | Per person | What you get | What it costs besides money |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scratch, 15-lb bird | $84 | $7.00 | 8 dishes, leftovers to Sunday | About 8 hours of work, one oven |
| Supermarket heat-and-serve | $155 to $215 | $13 to $18 | Turkey, 4 sides, rolls, pie, all reheated | A pickup slot and an order deadline |
| Restaurant prix fixe | $540 to $720 | $45 to $60 | No dishes, no oven, no timing | A reservation and no leftovers |
| Potluck, host does bird and two sides | $40 host | $3.50 host, $15 to $25 per guest dish | More food than twelve people eat | Coordinating who brings what |

The heat-and-serve number needs explaining. Most packages are built for 8 to 10, so a table of twelve buys the 10-person meal at $109 to $149 and adds a bone-in breast at $25 to $40 plus an extra side. That addition is where the per-person figure climbs from $11 to $17.

Twelve is the break-even size. Below eight, the prepared meal is competitive, because the labor saved is a full day and the food cost gap is only $40 to $60. Above sixteen, the packages stop scaling.

## The Deadline Decides More Than the Price

Six days out is where this decision actually gets made, and it is the part people leave until Tuesday.

- **Friday, November 20:** call the catering desk. Most supermarket programs close orders five to seven days before the holiday, so a twelve-person meal booked today gets a Tuesday or Wednesday pickup. The 4 p.m. Wednesday slots are gone by Monday.
- **Saturday, November 21:** last comfortable day to buy a frozen bird. A 15-pounder needs four days in the refrigerator at four to five pounds a day, which is the pace in our [thaw guide](/blog/how-long-to-thaw-turkey-thanksgiving/). Buy it Sunday and you are thawing in a cold-water bath for six hours.
- **Monday, November 23:** dry goods. Canned pumpkin, fried onions, stuffing cubes, and evaporated milk are the four items that disappear first and keep longest, so buy them early without risk.
- **Tuesday, November 24:** pies and cranberry sauce. Both hold two days in the fridge, and the sauce is better on day two.
- **Wednesday, November 25:** potatoes peeled and sitting in cold water, stuffing mixed to the bread stage but not baked, bird dry-brined and uncovered on the bottom shelf.
- **Thursday, November 26:** a 15-pound bird at 13 minutes a pound goes in at 8:15 a.m. for a 2 p.m. sit-down, allowing 3 hours 15 minutes plus 30 minutes of rest. The [cooking time calculator](/cooking-time-calculator/) works that backward from your serve time.

## If Twenty-Four People Show Up

Scratch scales better than anything else on the list. Doubling to twenty-four guests means two 12-pound birds at $18 each, ten pounds of potatoes, and a second pie, which is about $150 and $6.25 a head. One 24-pounder is the wrong purchase: it needs five hours in the oven and five days to thaw.

The prepared-meal route does not scale at all. Two 10-person packages at $109 to $149 each is $218 to $298, and you now own two trays of the same four sides.

## Where to Cut Without Anyone Noticing

- Skip the fresh bean casserole and roast two pounds of carrots at $1.29 a pound. Saves $6.
- Buy a 20-pound bird instead of a 15 and serve it in two settings. The extra $7 buys Saturday lunch.
- Make one pie and one pumpkin custard in a 9x13 pan. The custard costs $4 less than a second pie and feeds more people.
- Buy butter in the one-pound block rather than four sticks. Same product, $1 to $2 less per pound.

## FAQ

### How much does Thanksgiving dinner cost for 10 people?

About $70, or $7 a head, for the scratch menu in the table above scaled down to a 13-pound bird and four pounds of potatoes. A supermarket heat-and-serve meal for ten runs $109 to $149, which is $11 to $15 per person.

### Is it cheaper to cook Thanksgiving dinner or buy it?

Cooking is roughly half the cost of a store-bought meal and a fifth of a restaurant. The $70 to $140 you save is payment for about eight hours of work and one oven running all morning. If your time that day is worth more than $20 an hour to you, the prepared meal is the rational buy.

### How much should I budget per person for Thanksgiving?

Budget $8 a head for scratch cooking, which leaves room for butter, cream, and a second pie. Budget $15 to $18 for a supermarket meal, and $50 to $60 for a restaurant once tax and tip are on the check.
