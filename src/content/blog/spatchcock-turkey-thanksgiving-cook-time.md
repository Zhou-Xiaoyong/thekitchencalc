## Thanksgiving Morning, 8:40, and the Bird Is Still Cold

Eighty minutes. A 14-pound turkey with the backbone out and the bird pressed flat roasts at 450°F in 70 to 80 minutes. The same bird cooked whole at 325°F needs 3 to 3¾ hours and holds the oven until 2:15.

That gap is the whole argument. Spatchcocking hands back two hours without a second oven, a fryer, or a trip to the deli counter.

## Time by Weight, and the Pan That Has to Fit

These times assume a fully thawed bird, an oven at 450°F, and the pan on a rack in the lower-middle position. Start checking at the early number.

| Bird | Time at 450°F | Oven on by, 1:30 dinner | Pan |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 to 11 lb | 55 to 65 min | 12:10 p.m. | 18x13 half sheet |
| 12 to 13 lb | 65 to 75 min | 11:55 a.m. | 18x13 half sheet |
| 14 to 15 lb | 70 to 85 min | 11:40 a.m. | 18x13, legs tucked in |
| 16 to 17 lb | 85 to 100 min | 11:20 a.m. | 21x15 rimmed pan |
| 18 lb and up | not this method | — | will not fit |

**The pan decides the bird.** A half sheet is 18 by 13 inches, and a flattened turkey eats every inch of it at 15 pounds. A 16-pounder needs a 21x15 pan, which still clears the walls of a 24-inch oven cavity. An 18-pound bird does not fit, and forcing it presses the legs against the door so they steam instead of brown.

In a convection oven, set 425°F and take 10 minutes off the range. Then trust the thermometer over the chart.

## Twelve Minutes, Shears, and One Good Crack

**Giblets first.** Reach in from the tail end before anything else. A paper bag of liver going into a 450°F oven is a smell you do not get back.

**Cut out the backbone.** Breast-side down, tail toward you. Cut through the ribs down one side of the spine, then the other, tail to neck. Follow the seam of fat where the ribs meet it. Save the backbone; it browns in a dry skillet in eight minutes and makes the gravy.

**Flatten it.** Breast-side up, both palms on the breastbone, press until it gives. It takes a real push. The bird should sit level with the legs splayed to the corners.

**Dry, then oil.** Pat until the skin is tacky rather than wet, then rub with neutral oil. Not butter. Milk solids scorch at around 350°F and turn bitter within 20 minutes at 450°F.

**Salt now.** One teaspoon of kosher salt per pound, over the skin and into the joints. A 24-hour dry brine is off the table at this hour, but surface salt still seasons the outer half inch.

**Protect the drippings.** A cup of stock under the rack, or a sliced onion and a carrot. Turkey fat hitting bare metal at 450°F carbonizes, and then the smoke alarm joins the meal.

## Why Flat Beats Whole

In a whole bird the thigh sits three inches from the surface and the breast sits one, so the cuts finish 30 to 40 degrees apart and you wait on one while the other dries out. Flatten it and every cut sits the same distance from the oven floor. Breast and thighs land within about 10 minutes of each other.

The skin gains too. No folded surfaces, no trapped moisture, more crackle. The trade is that it browns early and fast.

Pull at 165°F in the breast and 175°F in the thickest part of the thigh. If the breast gets there first, foil over the breast only and give the legs 10 more minutes. Rest 25 minutes, loosely tented.

## The Oven-Space Win, on the Clock

This matters more than the roast time. A 14-pounder out at 12:20 for a 1:30 dinner frees the oven for the sides that usually get crammed into the last 40 minutes.

- **12:20 p.m.** Bird out, resting. Oven down to 400°F.
- **12:25.** Dressing into a 9x13 with a cup of stock, 35 minutes.
- **1:00.** Dressing out, green bean casserole in for 25.
- **1:10.** Rolls beside it, 12 minutes.
- **1:22.** Everything out. Carve at 1:25.

## If It Is Already 2 P.M.

A 14-pounder entering at 2:00 is done at 3:15, which works for a 4 p.m. dinner and not a 3 p.m. one. Past that, change the cut instead of the clock.

- **Break it into parts.** Breast halves at 425°F for 50 to 60 minutes, whole legs 15 minutes longer, wings 35. Two pans, one oven.
- **Buy a bone-in breast.** Six to seven pounds, 2 hours at 350°F, feeds eight.
- **Two rotisserie chickens.** One per four guests, carved at the table.

A bird still frozen solid rules out all of this, shears included, because they will not go through a solid rib cage. ## FAQ

### How long does a spatchcocked turkey take to cook?

At 450°F, 55 to 65 minutes for a 10-pounder, 70 to 85 for 14 pounds, 85 to 100 for 16. Ovens lie about their own temperature, so start checking with an instant-read thermometer at the 50-minute mark.

### What internal temperature should a spatchcocked turkey reach?

165°F in the breast, 175°F in the thigh. The higher number for legs is texture, not safety: leg meat stays chewy and pink near the bone below 175°F. On a flattened bird both cuts arrive close together, so one check usually settles it.

### Can you spatchcock a turkey the night before?

Yes, and it is the better schedule. Backbone out Wednesday, salted, uncovered on a rack in the fridge overnight. Dry air tightens the skin, which crisps faster at 450°F, and the salt reaches the meat instead of sitting on it. Add 5 minutes for a cold bird going straight from fridge to oven.
