## Yes, and a 16-Pound Bird Needs Six Hours

At 7:10 on Thanksgiving morning the bird in the sink is still hard enough to knock on. A cold-water bath cannot save a 16-pounder before noon, so put it in the oven.

A turkey goes into a 325°F oven straight from the freezer. USDA's guidance is to allow about 50 percent more time than a thawed bird, which turns the usual 3¾ to 4¼ hours for 16 pounds into 5¾ to 6¼. Same bird, same oven, two extra hours.

That answer only works if the oven is on now. Here is the clock, the three places a frozen start goes wrong, and the mid-morning fallback.

## Where the 50 Percent Comes From

Heat crosses the bird twice on the way in. First it melts ice, then it warms water toward 165°F. Melting absorbs a large amount of energy before the temperature moves at all, which is why a frozen roast sits near 30°F for its first hour and then climbs.

So the correction is half again the time you would have used, not double. Multiply your thawed number by 1.5 and round up.

| Bird | Thawed at 325°F | From frozen | Oven on by, for a 2 p.m. dinner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 lb | 2¾ to 3 hr | 4¼ to 4½ hr | 9:10 am |
| 13 lb | 3 to 3¾ hr | 4½ to 5¾ hr | 8:20 am |
| 16 lb | 3¾ to 4¼ hr | 5¾ to 6¼ hr | 7:30 am |
| 19 lb | 4¼ to 4½ hr | 6¼ to 6¾ hr | 7:00 am |
| 22 lb | 4½ to 5 hr | 6¾ to 7½ hr | 6:25 am |

Those start times subtract 30 minutes of rest from a 2:00 serve time and use the middle of each range, so one bird running long still lands late rather than ruined. The [cooking time calculator](/cooking-time-calculator/) handles the arithmetic for a different serve time or a bigger bird.

A frozen 22-pounder is the outer limit of this trick in a home oven: 6¾ hours or more, and a rack low enough that the top of the bird clears the element. Two 12-pound birds, both from frozen, finish sooner.

## Three Things a Frozen Bird Does Differently

**The skin browns about two hours before the meat is done.** The skin is at oven temperature within 40 minutes while the thigh is still near 60°F. Tent the whole bird loosely with foil for the first two-thirds of the cook, then uncover it for the last 45 minutes to crisp. Leave the tent sealed and you steam the skin gray.

**The breast overcooks while you wait on the thigh.** Frozen birds finish outside-in, so the white meat can read 180°F while the thigh sits at 150°F. At the 90-minute mark, lay a double strip of foil over the breast only, and check the thigh from hour four onward.

**The cavity is the coldest part, and it stays cold longest.** Do not stuff a bird that started frozen. The stuffing sits against ice and cannot reach 165°F before the meat around it dries out. Cook the dressing in a 9x13 pan at 350°F for 40 minutes instead, and add a cup of stock so it does not set like a brick.

One more: keep the oven at 325°F. Raising it to 350°F to save time browns the skin to mahogany while the thigh is still at 130°F, and the gap between white and dark meat widens instead of closing.

## The Giblet Bag Is the Only Real Obstacle

Frozen birds ship with the neck and giblets in a paper or plastic bag inside the cavity, and the bag will not slide out of a solid bird.

Run cool water into the cavity for five minutes. That melts the ice touching the bag without thawing the meat, and most bags come free with a firm pull. If yours will not move, leave it. Roast the bird as planned and pull the bag with tongs once the cavity has opened up, around the two-hour mark, when it lifts out in one piece.

The plastic pop-up timer that comes with the bird is worth ignoring. It is set to fire near 180°F in the breast, which is 15 degrees past where the thigh should be pulled. Use an instant-read thermometer in the thigh, off the bone, and confirm 165°F.

## Your Morning, in Order

The bird has a two-hour head start on everything else, and that reshapes the day.

- **6:45 am.** Oven to 325°F, rack in the lower third. Pull the bird out of the freezer now, not at 7:20, and get the wrapper off over the sink.
- **7:00 am.** Cool water in the cavity, giblet bag out or abandoned, bird breast up on a flat rack in a pan with 2-inch sides. A cup of water in the pan keeps the drippings from burning during the long roast.
- **7:30 am.** Bird in, foil tented loosely over the top. Shut the door and leave it alone for three hours. Every peek costs 15 minutes of oven heat.
- **9:00 am.** Foil strip over the breast. This is also the moment to peel potatoes and mix the dressing, because the oven is booked until 1:30.
- **11:00 am.** First thigh check, knowing it will still read under 140°F. Nothing is wrong.
- **12:00 pm.** Uncover the bird for the last stretch of browning. Start the rolls and the green beans in the second oven or the toaster oven, since the main oven stays shut.
- **1:15 to 1:45 pm.** Thigh hits 165°F. Out, rest 30 minutes, carve at 2:00.

Guests at 12:30 get a house that smells right and a host who is not babysitting a cooler of cold water.

## If It Is Already 10 A.M.

A 16-pound bird entering the oven at 10:00 cannot be done by 2:00 by any method that keeps it whole. Cold water runs 30 minutes a pound, which is eight hours for that bird, so that door is closed too.

Cut it up. A heavy chef's knife or kitchen shears will part a partly frozen turkey at the joints, and pieces roast at 400°F in about an hour.

| Cut | Weight | Time at 400°F | Pull at |
|---|---|---|---|
| Breast halves, bone-in | 2½ to 3 lb each | 50 to 60 min | 165°F |
| Whole legs | 1½ to 2 lb each | 55 to 65 min | 175°F |
| Wings | 6 to 8 oz | 35 to 40 min | 175°F |

Put the legs in 15 minutes before the breasts, since dark meat wants the higher finish temperature anyway. Skin-side up on a sheet pan, salt and pepper, no foil. The bird will not carve into the Norman Rockwell table centerpiece, and it will taste the same.

If even that is too slow, a bone-in breast roast from the supermarket case cooks in 1¾ hours from thawed and about 2½ from frozen, and two rotisserie chickens from the deli cover the plate for anyone who gave up at noon. ## FAQ

### Can you put a frozen turkey directly into the oven?

Yes. USDA allows cooking a turkey from frozen with no thawing step, provided the thigh and breast both reach 165°F, and the stuffing too if there is any. The bird takes roughly 50 percent longer than a thawed one and the skin browns early, so tent it with foil.

### How much longer does a frozen turkey take to cook?

Half again as long. A 16-pound bird that needs 3¾ to 4¼ hours thawed needs 5¾ to 6¼ hours frozen, and a 20-pounder goes from about 4½ hours to nearly 7. Add another 30 minutes if the oven door gets opened repeatedly or the pan has sides deeper than 2 inches.

### Is a frozen turkey safe to eat if the outside looks done early?

Yes, as long as the thermometer says so. Browning is a surface reaction and says nothing about the thigh, which lags by 30 to 50 degrees on a frozen start. Cover the breast with foil when it darkens and keep cooking until the thigh reads 165°F.
