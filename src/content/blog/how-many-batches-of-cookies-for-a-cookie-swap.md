## Six Dozen Needs 2.4 Batches, So Bake Three

The group text says bring six dozen. That is 72 cookies, and the standard chocolate chip batch on the back of the bag weighs 1,263 grams. Portion it at 42 grams and you get 30 cookies.

Seventy-two divided by 30 is 2.4. Nobody bakes four-tenths of a batch, so the real answer is three: 90 cookies, 18 left over for the tin on the counter.

## One Batch Is 1,263 Grams, and That Is the Only Number You Need

That weight comes from a 2¼-cup formula: 270 grams of flour, 227 grams of butter, 150 grams of granulated sugar, 165 grams of packed brown sugar, two eggs, a teaspoon each of soda and salt, and 340 grams of chips. Weigh the finished dough once and you can size any swap from it.

Scoop size is the other half of the math.

| Scoop | Dough per cookie | Cookies per batch | Spread | Bake at 350°F |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 tablespoon | 18 g | 70 | 2½ in | 8 to 9 min |
| 2 tablespoons | 30 g | 42 | 2¾ in | 9 to 11 min |
| 3 tablespoons | 42 g | 30 | 3¼ in | 11 to 13 min |
| ¼ cup | 55 g | 23 | 3¾ in | 13 to 15 min |

Swap cookies travel in a box and get handled by strangers, so 42 grams is the size that survives the trip. A 30-gram cookie reads as a sample. The [Recipe Servings Calculator](/recipe-servings-calculator/) scales by total dough weight, which is the only workable unit for a food with no servings.

## So How Many Batches of Cookies for a Cookie Swap?

Six dozen per person is the standard rule, and it does not change when the swap grows. A twelve-person swap means twelve bakers each bringing six dozen, not twelve times the work for you. The count below is what one baker bakes.

| What you owe | Cookies | Dough needed | Batches at 42 g | Butter | Chips |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 dozen | 48 | 2,016 g | 1.6, bake 2 | 4 sticks | 2 bags |
| 6 dozen | 72 | 3,024 g | 2.4, bake 3 | 6 sticks | 3 bags |
| 8 dozen | 96 | 4,032 g | 3.2, bake 4 | 8 sticks | 4 bags |
| 10 dozen | 120 | 5,040 g | 4.0, bake 4 | 8 sticks | 4 bags |

Ten dozen is the point where the arithmetic finally lands on a whole number, and the point where most home ovens stop being the bottleneck.

## Round Up, Because the Fraction Costs an Egg

Staying at 2.4 batches means measuring 0.8 of an egg. That is 40 grams of beaten egg, which works, and which nobody wants to do at 9 p.m. on a Tuesday.

The multiplier that keeps the shopping list clean is three. Three batches use six eggs, six sticks of butter, and exactly three 12-ounce bags of chips. One batch is two eggs and two sticks, so anything in multiples of three buys whole packages with nothing left over.

Two batches also work, if the swap is four dozen and you want a spare dozen for the family.

## Your Mixer Stops at Two Batches

A 5-quart stand mixer holds 4.7 liters. Two batches of this dough weigh 2,526 grams and take up about 2.4 liters, so the bowl runs half full and the paddle never disappears.

Three batches weigh 3,789 grams, roughly 3.6 liters, or three-quarters of the bowl. The creaming stage survives that. Adding 810 grams of flour does not, because the dry flour sits on top of a full bowl and the first turn of the paddle throws it over the rim.

Mix three in two bowls: a double and a single, then combine. Or cream all three in the big bowl, divide the butter mixture in half, and finish each half with its own flour. One extra bowl, one less mixing cycle.

## The Sheet Pan Sets the Length of the Afternoon

A half sheet pan measures 18 by 13 inches, which is 234 square inches of floor. A 42-gram cookie spreads to about 3¼ inches, so a 3½-inch pitch gives five across and three down. Fifteen cookies per pan.

| Cookies needed | Pans at 42 g | Oven loads, 2 pans at a time | Oven minutes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 48 | 4 | 2 | 26 |
| 72 | 5 | 3 | 39 |
| 90 | 6 | 3 | 39 |
| 120 | 8 | 4 | 52 |

Ninety cookies is three oven loads, not five, because two pans fit side by side on one rack and most ovens hold two racks. Rotate the pans front to back and top to bottom at the halfway mark, and add two minutes for every time you open the door.

At 30 grams the same pan holds 24, which cuts a 72-cookie job to two loads.

## Chill Portions, Not the Bowl

Cold dough spreads less and holds a rounder edge. The mistake is chilling 3.8 kilograms of dough in one mass, where the center takes four hours to reach 38°F and the outside dries.

Scoop first. Set the portions on a sheet pan, cover, and give them 30 minutes in the refrigerator. They cool through in that time because each one is only 42 grams.

Chilled dough bakes about a quarter inch narrower and needs one to two extra minutes, which is why the table above runs 11 to 13 instead of 9 to 11.

## Which Doughs Survive the Multiplier

| Dough | Triples cleanly? | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Chocolate chip | Yes | Nothing. Weigh the dough and portion |
| Roll-out sugar cookies | Yes | Chill between batches, flour the bench lightly |
| Shortbread | Yes | No eggs, so no fraction to round |
| Oatmeal raisin | Yes | Let it rest 20 minutes before scooping, oats need hydration |
| Gingerbread | Mostly | Molasses makes a triple batch sticky, add flour a tablespoon at a time |
| Macarons, meringues | No | Bake one batch at a time, the meringue does not hold |

Gingerbread is the one that catches people, because the dough looks right in the bowl and then welds itself to the counter. A triple batch takes about 2 extra tablespoons of flour, added after the molasses.

## Bake on the 21st, Not the 24th

The oven on the 25th belongs to the ham, which wants 325°F for two hours and then a blast at 375°F for the glaze. The 24th belongs to pies, rolls, and the stuffing that has to go in after the bird comes out.

Cookie dough does not care about that schedule. Portion it into balls, freeze them solid on a sheet pan for 45 minutes, then bag them. They keep three months, and they bake straight from frozen at 350°F with two to three extra minutes on the clock.

Baked cookies also freeze, up to a month in an airtight tin, but anything iced or sugared will sweat on the way back to room temperature. Freeze the dough if the swap is more than a week out.

## FAQ

### How many batches of cookies for a cookie swap?

Six dozen, the standard swap amount, is 2.4 batches of a 1,263-gram dough at a 42-gram scoop. Bake three batches for 90 cookies, which covers the swap plus a spare dozen.

### How many cookies does one batch of cookie dough make?

It depends on the scoop. A 1,263-gram batch gives 70 cookies at 18 grams, 42 at 30 grams, 30 at 42 grams, and 23 at 55 grams. Weigh one portion before you commit to a count.

### Can you triple a cookie recipe?

Yes, and cookies are the easiest thing in the kitchen to triple, because eggs and leavening scale linearly. Mix it in two bowls if your mixer is 5 quarts. Three batches of dough fill the bowl to three-quarters and the flour will not fold in without spilling.
