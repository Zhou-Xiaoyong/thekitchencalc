## How Many Teaspoons in a Tablespoon? The Short Answer

**3 teaspoons = 1 tablespoon.** That is the US customary standard, and it is also the UK standard. If you remember nothing else from this page, remember the number three — half a tablespoon is 1½ teaspoons, a third of a tablespoon is 1 teaspoon, and a quarter of a tablespoon is ¾ teaspoon.

That single ratio is the difference between a recipe that tastes right and one that tastes flat. A tablespoon of baking powder instead of a teaspoon is a triple dose, and it will make your muffins collapse. Spoon measurements are small, but the errors are large in percentage terms.

| Tablespoons | Teaspoons | Fluid ounces | Milliliters |
|-------------|-----------|--------------|-------------|
| 1 tbsp | 3 tsp | ½ fl oz | 14.79 mL |
| ¾ tbsp | 2¼ tsp | ⅜ fl oz | 11.1 mL |
| ⅔ tbsp | 2 tsp | ⅓ fl oz | 9.86 mL |
| ½ tbsp | 1½ tsp | ¼ fl oz | 7.39 mL |
| ⅓ tbsp | 1 tsp | ⅙ fl oz | 4.93 mL |
| ¼ tbsp | ¾ tsp | ⅛ fl oz | 3.70 mL |
| ⅛ tbsp | ⅜ tsp | 1/16 fl oz | 1.85 mL |

Measured the other direction: **1 teaspoon = ⅓ tablespoon**. Two teaspoons = ⅔ tablespoon. Four teaspoons = 1⅓ tablespoons. There is no way to express 1 teaspoon as a clean fraction of a tablespoon beyond that one-third, which is exactly why recipe writers who halve carefully usually switch to teaspoons for anything below a tablespoon.

## How Many Teaspoons Are in a Cup, a Half Cup, and a Quarter Cup?

Once you know a tablespoon is 3 teaspoons, the rest of the US volume chain falls into place. There are 16 tablespoons in a cup, so there are **48 teaspoons in a cup**.

| Amount | Tablespoons | Teaspoons |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| 1 cup | 16 tbsp | 48 tsp |
| ¾ cup | 12 tbsp | 36 tsp |
| ⅔ cup | 10 tbsp + 2 tsp | 32 tsp |
| ½ cup | 8 tbsp | 24 tsp |
| ⅓ cup | 5 tbsp + 1 tsp | 16 tsp |
| ¼ cup | 4 tbsp | 12 tsp |
| ⅛ cup | 2 tbsp | 6 tsp |

That ⅓ cup line is the one that trips people up. There is no such thing as "5⅓ tablespoons" on a measuring spoon, so the convention is to measure 5 tablespoons and then add 1 teaspoon. Same volume, measurable tools.

## The One Exception: Australia Uses 4 Teaspoons per Tablespoon

Australian recipes use a **20 mL tablespoon** and a **5 mL teaspoon**, so in Australia **1 tablespoon = 4 teaspoons**. Everywhere else — the US, the UK, Canada, New Zealand, and most of Europe — a tablespoon is 15 mL and the ratio is 3:1.

How to spot it: an Australian recipe will usually specify metric (**20 mL**) or list ingredients in grams. If a recipe looks suspiciously heavy on tablespoons and the results come out too salty, too boozy, or too sweet by a third, you probably used an Australian tablespoon measurement in a 15 mL world, or the reverse.

A practical safeguard: for anything with a strong flavour profile — salt, baking powder, vanilla, chilli, yeast — measure in grams or millilitres when the recipe is ambiguous. Spoon sizes vary, but millilitres do not.

## Teaspoons, Tablespoons, and the Milliliter Bridge

The size of the spoon differs by region, but the millilitre value is fixed and unambiguous. This is the table to use when a recipe mixes spoon units with metric units.

| Spoon | US / UK / Canada | Australia |
|-------|------------------|-----------|
| 1 teaspoon | 5 mL | 5 mL |
| 1 tablespoon | 15 mL | 20 mL |
| 1 tablespoon in teaspoons | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |

Note that a teaspoon is 5 mL everywhere — only the tablespoon changes. That is why conversion charts that list "1 tsp = 5 mL" are safe internationally, while "1 tbsp = 15 mL" needs a check if the recipe is Australian.

## What Is a Tablespoon of Ingredients in Grams?

Volume-to-weight conversion is where spoons get genuinely unreliable, because the same 15 mL of two different ingredients can differ in weight by a factor of two. These are the US-standard figures:

| Ingredient | 1 tbsp | 1 tsp |
|------------|--------|-------|
| Water / milk | 15 g | 5 g |
| All-purpose flour | 7.5 g | 2.5 g |
| Granulated sugar | 12.5 g | 4.2 g |
| Brown sugar (packed) | 13.75 g | 4.6 g |
| Butter | 14.2 g | 4.7 g |
| Vegetable oil | 13.6 g | 4.5 g |
| Honey | 21.25 g | 7.1 g |
| Table salt | 18 g | 6 g |
| Baking powder | 12 g | 4 g |
| Cocoa powder | 5.4 g | 1.8 g |

Two lessons hide in that table. First, a teaspoon of salt weighs more than a teaspoon of baking powder by half, so "1 tsp of dry stuff" is not a real unit. Second, honey is half again as heavy as the same volume of oil — the one case where the density difference is large enough to break a recipe outright.

Scoop style matters too. A dipped-and-swept tablespoon of flour is about 7.5 g, but a heaped or packed one can hit 10–12 g. ## How to Convert Teaspoons to Tablespoons Without the Right Spoon

You are mid-recipe, the tablespoon is in the dishwasher, and all you have is a teaspoon. Here is the ladder:

- **Need 1 tbsp?** Use 3 tsp.
- **Need ½ tbsp?** Use 1½ tsp, or ½ tsp three times.
- **Need ¼ cup?** Use 4 tbsp, or 12 tsp.
- **Need ⅓ cup?** Use 5 tbsp + 1 tsp, or 16 tsp.
- **Need a pinch and only have a ¼ tsp?** A pinch is roughly half of a ¼ tsp, so eyeball half of that spoon.

Two habits make spoon measures more accurate:

1. **Level dry ingredients with the flat edge of a knife.** A heaped teaspoon of baking powder is roughly 1⅔ teaspoons — a 67% error.
2. **Use a liquid measuring cup for anything over 2 tablespoons.** Spoon-by-spoon measuring of oil compounds rounding errors fast.

## Scaling Recipes That Use Spoons

Once you start doubling and halving, spoons get awkward. Doubling 1⅔ teaspoons gives 3⅓ teaspoons, which is conventionally written as 1 tablespoon + ⅓ teaspoon. Halving 1 tablespoon gives 1½ teaspoons, which is clean.

The short version:

- **Halving:** a tablespoon becomes 1½ tsp; a teaspoon becomes ½ tsp; ½ tsp becomes ¼ tsp.
- **Doubling:** 3 tsp of anything becomes 2 tbsp; 1 tsp becomes 2 tsp.
- **Tripling:** 1 tsp becomes 1 tbsp. Convenient, because the 3:1 ratio lines up perfectly.

When you scale a whole recipe, leavening and salt should scale exactly — but seasonings like herbs, chilli, and strong spices often want 1.25× instead of 2×, because your palate does not scale linearly with volume.
