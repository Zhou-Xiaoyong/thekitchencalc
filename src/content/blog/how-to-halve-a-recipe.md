## Why Halving a Recipe Is Trickier Than It Sounds

You'd think dividing everything by two would be straightforward. And for savory cooking, it usually is. But baking? That's where things get messy.

Halving a recipe isn't always as simple as dividing every number by two. Some ingredients don't scale linearly, and others are just plain hard to measure in small amounts.

In this guide, we'll walk you through exactly how to halve any recipe, common pitfalls to avoid, and give you a free tool to do the math for you.

## The Simple Math: Halving Dry and Liquid Ingredients

For most ingredients, halving is straightforward:

- 1 cup → ½ cup
- 2 tablespoons → 1 tablespoon
- 4 teaspoons → 2 teaspoons
- 1 pound → 8 ounces

But what about odd numbers? Here's a quick reference:

| Original Amount | Half Amount |
|----------------|-------------|
| ⅓ cup | 2 tablespoons + 2 teaspoons |
| ⅔ cup | ⅓ cup |
| ¾ cup | 6 tablespoons |
| ¼ cup | 2 tablespoons |
| 1 tablespoon | 1½ teaspoons |
| 1 teaspoon | ½ teaspoon |
| ½ teaspoon | ¼ teaspoon |

> **Pro tip:** When halving a recipe, use a kitchen scale for the most accurate results. Weighing ingredients is far more precise than measuring cups, especially for flour and sugar.

## Ingredients That Don't Scale Perfectly

Some ingredients can't just be divided by two. Here are the tricky ones:

### Eggs
Halving an egg is messy. Here's what to do:
- **For baking**: Crack an egg, whisk it lightly, and use half by weight (about 25g per large egg).
- **For savory dishes**: If the recipe calls for 1 egg and you're halving, you can usually still use a whole egg without disaster — especially for binding.
- **Alternative**: Use one egg yolk instead of a whole egg for richness without the extra white.

### Baking Soda and Baking Powder
These leavening agents are powerful. Too much = soapy taste. Too little = dense baked goods.

When halving:
- 1 teaspoon baking soda → ½ teaspoon
- 2 teaspoons baking powder → 1 teaspoon

**Important**: For recipes that are heavily dependent on leavening (like cakes), be very precise with your measurements. A digital scale that measures in grams is ideal.

### Spices and Seasonings
Salt, pepper, and spices don't always scale linearly. When halving a savory recipe:
- Start with **slightly less than half** the salt and spices
- Taste and adjust as you go
- It's easier to add more salt than to fix an over-salted dish

### Yeast
Yeast can be tricky. For most bread recipes, you can halve yeast, but:
- The dough may rise more slowly
- You'll get slightly different flavor development
- For very small batches, consider using a little more yeast than the exact half

## How to Halve Baking Recipes: 5 Pro Tips

Baking is chemistry, and scaling requires care. Follow these rules:

### 1. Use Weight Measurements Whenever Possible
Cups are inconsistent — a cup of flour can weigh anywhere from 120g to 150g depending on how you pack it. Weighing removes all guesswork.

### 2. Adjust Pan Size
If you're halving a cake or casserole recipe, you'll need a smaller pan. As a rule of thumb:
- Halved recipe ≈ 70% of the original pan diameter
- 9-inch round → 6-inch round
- 9x13 inch → 8x8 inch (approximately)

### 3. Don't Halve the Salt in Yeast Doughs
Salt regulates yeast activity. If you halve the salt but not the yeast (or even if you do), you might get a too-fast rise and off-flavors. Stick close to the original salt-to-flour ratio.

### 4. Watch Cooking Time Carefully
Smaller portions cook faster. Start checking for doneness **25-30% earlier** than the original recipe states.

### 5. Temperature Matters
Keep the oven temperature the same. Don't try to "speed things up" by turning up the heat — you'll burn the outside before the inside cooks.

## Common Mistakes When Halving Recipes

### ❌ Mistake #1: Rounding Too Aggressively
"Eh, close enough" works for soups and stews. Not for cakes and bread. Be precise.

### ❌ Mistake #2: Forgetting to Adjust Pan Size
Using a full-size pan for a half recipe means thinner food, which cooks faster and may dry out.

### ❌ Mistake #3: Halving Everything Blindly
Eggs, spices, and leavening sometimes need judgment. Use the guidelines above.

### ❌ Mistake #4: Not Tasting
For savory recipes, always taste and adjust seasoning at the end, especially after scaling.

## Final Thought

Halving a recipe is a great skill — it means you can cook for one or two people without wasting food. The key is to be precise with baking, flexible with savory cooking, and always taste as you go.
