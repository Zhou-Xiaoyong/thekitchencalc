## Six Cups for Twelve, Not Four

Thanksgiving needs one-third cup of gravy per person, and that assumes a second pass over the potatoes. Twelve guests means six cups.

Most people make four. Four cups is one gravy boat, and one gravy boat runs dry before the stuffing gets halfway around the table.

The [holiday meal planner](/holiday-meal-planner/) multiplies guests by 0.33 to reach the same number. What follows is how to turn six cups into a pot of gravy without guessing at flour.

## The One-Third Cup Is a First-Pass Number

A dinner plate takes about a quarter cup: two tablespoons over the turkey, two over the potatoes. That leaves a third of the table wanting more, and the last four people in line scraping the boat.

A third of a cup covers the first pass with a little left over. The half again on top of it is what actually gets eaten, because gravy is the cheapest thing on the table to make twice and the most noticed thing to run out of.

Some people skip gravy entirely. If two of your twelve are among them, make five cups and keep a cup of stock on the stove to loosen whatever is left in the pot.

## Cups, Stock, and Roux by Guest Count

One tablespoon of butter and one tablespoon of flour thicken one cup of liquid into a gravy that coats the back of a spoon. That single ratio sets the whole table.

| Guests | Gravy to make | Turkey stock | Butter | Flour | Smallest saucepan |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 3 cups | 3 cups | 3 tbsp | 3 tbsp | 2 qt |
| 8 | 4 cups | 4 cups | 4 tbsp | 4 tbsp | 2 qt |
| 10 | 5 cups | 5 cups | 5 tbsp | 5 tbsp | 3 qt |
| 12 | 6 cups | 6 cups | 6 tbsp | 6 tbsp | 3 qt |
| 16 | 8 cups | 8 cups | 8 tbsp (1 stick) | 8 tbsp | 4 qt |
| 20 | 10 cups | 10 cups | 10 tbsp | 10 tbsp | 5 qt |

Six cups is a quart and a half. A three-quart saucepan holds it with enough headroom to whisk hard, and whisking hard is how gravy stays smooth.

Two adjustments to the ratio:

- **Thinner gravy**, for pouring over rice or an open-faced sandwich: 2 teaspoons of flour per cup.
- **Thicker gravy**, the kind that sits on the plate instead of spreading into the cranberry sauce: 1½ tablespoons per cup, bubbled for five minutes.

Cook the roux two full minutes before any liquid goes in. It should smell like toasted bread. Roux that still smells raw tastes like paste, and no amount of salt fixes that.

## Your Turkey Gives a Cup and a Half, at Best

A 15-pound bird leaves one to one and a half cups of pan liquid. Spoon off the fat and you are closer to a cup. A 20-pounder gives about two.

So five of those six cups are stock, and the neck and giblets are where it comes from. Simmer them in four cups of water with half an onion for 45 minutes at a bare simmer and you get roughly two cups of strong broth. Add a quart of boxed stock and the arithmetic closes.

Two details worth the trouble:

- **Chill the drippings** for twenty minutes and the fat lifts off as a solid disc. A tablespoon of that turkey fat can replace a tablespoon of butter in the roux, which is where the roasted flavor really lives.
- **Deglaze the roasting pan** with a cup of the stock over medium heat, scraping the brown bits loose, then strain it into the pot. That step is worth more than any seasoning you could add later.

## Make the Base Tuesday, Finish It Thursday

Roux plus stock, no drippings, keeps three days in the refrigerator and three months frozen in a quart container. Drippings go in on the day, because the roasted notes flatten overnight in a cold fridge.

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| Sunday or Monday | Simmer neck and giblets 45 minutes, strain, chill the stock |
| Tuesday | Cook the roux, add stock, simmer 5 minutes, cool uncovered 20 minutes, lid, refrigerate |
| Thursday, 90 minutes out | Reheat the base in the 3-quart pan, whisking |
| Thursday, 60 minutes out | Whisk in the strained, defatted drippings, simmer 5 minutes |
| Thursday, 30 minutes out | Taste for salt, move to a slow cooker on warm or a preheated thermos |

The reheat is where thin gravy gets fixed. If it loosens too much once the drippings are in, whisk a teaspoon of flour into a tablespoon of cold water and stir that in at a simmer.

## Gravy Is the First Thing on the Table to Turn

It is a liquid, it sits at room temperature, and every guest puts a used spoon back into it. Two hours is the outside limit, and one hour if the room is above 90°F. The [food safety temperature guide](/food-safety-temperature-guide/) puts a number on the whole table.

Keep the surplus in the pot on low rather than in the boat. Reheat anything that has been out to 165°F, stirring, and let it bubble a full minute instead of waiting for the edge to steam.

## Flour, Cornstarch, or Instant Flour

Gravy sits in a warmer for two hours on Thanksgiving, and that eliminates one of the three.

- **Flour roux.** Opaque, savory, holds up to a long warm hold, and reheats without separating. Six tablespoons each of butter and flour for six cups.
- **Cornstarch.** Glossy and translucent at one tablespoon per cup, but it needs a full minute at a boil to activate and it thins after a few hours of heat. Fine for gravy made at the last minute and served at once. It also breaks if you freeze it.
- **Instant flour.** Whisked straight into hot stock, no lumps, no roux. The body is thin, so it works as a patch on gravy that came out loose, not as the plan.

For a crowd, roux is the answer. It is also the only one of the three that can be made on Tuesday.

## Salt at the End, Always

A brined bird makes salty drippings. A [5% wet brine](/blog/turkey-brine-salt-ratio/) pushes salt all the way through the meat, and some of it comes back out into the pan.

Build the base with no salt at all, taste after the drippings go in, then add a quarter teaspoon at a time. In six cups of gravy a quarter teaspoon is a real change, and a full teaspoon is a ruined pot.

## FAQ

### How much gravy do I need for 20 guests?

Ten cups, which is two and a half quarts. That takes 10 tablespoons each of butter and flour, 10 cups of stock, and a 5-quart pot. Ten cups gives every guest a third of a cup with half again in reserve.

### Can I make Thanksgiving gravy ahead of time?

Yes, up to three days for the roux and stock base, or three months if you freeze it. Hold the drippings until Thursday, add them during the reheat, then simmer five minutes and salt to taste.

### How long can gravy sit out on the table?

Two hours, or one hour if the room is warmer than 90°F. After that, reheat it to 165°F and let it bubble a minute before it goes back out. Gravy that has been out longer than four hours should be discarded.
