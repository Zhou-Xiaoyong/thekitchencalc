## Nine Pounds for Twelve at the Christmas Table

A Christmas ham for twelve people is a 9-pound bone-in half, reheated at 325°F for about 2 hours and pulled at 140°F. That is 12 ounces of raw ham per seat and roughly 8 ounces on the plate.

That number catches people both ways. A 15-pound whole ham for ten guests means ham through January. A 6-pound boneless roast at a table of twelve empties before the second pass.

## Bone-In Ham Loses a Third Before the Plate

A bone-in half is roughly two-thirds meat. The shank bone runs its length and a fat cap covers one side. Nine pounds yields about 5.9 pounds of sliceable meat, or 94 ounces.

A holiday plate holds 5 to 7 ounces next to the potatoes and green beans, so 7.8 ounces each covers the first serving and the person who comes back for a cold slice while the pie is cut.

Boneless yields closer to 90 percent, so the buy weight drops by a third even as the price per pound climbs. Spiral-cut hams lose a little extra to the pre-cuts, since the blades open the surface and more outer meat dries.

## Ten Minutes a Pound Is Right Because the Ham Is Already Cooked

Almost every ham in a US supermarket is fully cooked. You are reheating, not roasting. The USDA time for a fully cooked whole ham is 10 to 15 minutes a pound at 325°F, and a half takes 12 to 15 because more surface meets the heat.

| Cut | Weight | Minutes per lb at 325°F | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fully cooked whole | 14 to 20 lb | 10 to 15 | 2½ to 5 hr |
| Fully cooked half | 7 to 10 lb | 12 to 15 | 1½ to 2½ hr |
| Small boneless roast | 3 to 5 lb | 30 to 35 | 1½ to 3 hr |

A 9-pound half at 13 minutes a pound is 117 minutes. Set the oven to 325°F rather than 350°F, since the hotter setting browns the outside before the middle warms.

The clock is a planning tool, not a doneness test. Push an instant-read thermometer into the thickest part, away from bone and fat, and start checking 20 minutes before the timer ends. A cook-before-eating ham is a different job: labeled "fresh," it runs 18 to 24 minutes a pound and must hit 145°F with a three-minute rest, per the [food safety temperature guide](/food-safety-temperature-guide/).

## The Label Decides Whether You Paid for Water

Four phrases on the label are legal categories, not marketing, and each tells you how much of the weight is brine.

- **Ham.** No added water.
- **Ham with natural juices.** Under 2 percent retained moisture.
- **Ham, water added.** Up to 10 percent solution, which on a 10-pound half is a pound of water.
- **Ham and water product.** More than 10 percent, and the label must state the percentage.

That last one is why two hams of the same size cook differently. A label reading 23 percent added solution makes a 10-pound ham a 7.7-pound ham, so buy up a size.

## A 9x13 Pan Holds a Ten-Pound Half

A 9-by-13 pan covers 117 square inches, and a 10-pound half measures about 11 inches long with a 7-inch cut face. It fits with an inch of clearance per side. A whole 15- to 18-pound ham wants a 16-by-12 roasting pan.

Put half a cup of water or cider in the pan so the drippings stay loose.

## Glaze in the Last 25 Minutes

Sugar burns before it caramelizes. Apply the glaze in the last 25 minutes and raise the oven to 375°F. Past that, brown sugar goes from lacquer to bitter in about 8 minutes.

For a 9- to 10-pound half, whisk ½ cup packed brown sugar, 2 tablespoons Dijon, 1 tablespoon cider vinegar, and ¼ teaspoon ground clove. Score the fat cap ¼ inch deep in a diamond pattern first so the sugar settles into the cuts. Skip the salt, since cured ham is already salty.

## FAQ

### How much ham per person for Christmas?

Three-quarters of a pound of bone-in ham per adult, or half a pound boneless. Twelve guests means a 9-pound half, which yields about 5.9 pounds of meat.

### How long do you cook a 10-pound spiral ham?

Two to two and a half hours at 325°F, at 12 to 15 minutes a pound, until the center reads 140°F. Glaze it in the last 25 minutes at 375°F.

### Can you eat a spiral ham without cooking it?

Yes. Fully cooked ham is safe cold from the refrigerator. The oven is for serving temperature and a glazed crust. Reheat to 140°F if it will sit on a buffet.
