## Fifteen Pounds for Twelve Guests

A twelve-person Thanksgiving wants a 15-pound bird. That is 20 ounces of raw turkey per seat and about 10 ounces of meat on the plate.

The gap between those numbers is why a 20-pound bird sometimes runs out at a table of sixteen and sometimes sends everyone home with a cooler. The [holiday meal planner](/holiday-meal-planner/) runs 1.25 pounds of whole turkey per adult, and for once the generic advice printed on every supermarket turkey tag happens to be right.

## Half the Bird Never Reaches the Plate

A raw bone-in turkey is roughly half bone, skin, and trim. Carve a 15-pounder well and you get 7 to 8 pounds of meat: two breast halves at about 2 pounds each, two legs at 12 to 14 ounces, two thighs at 10 to 12, and wings that mostly go into the stockpot.

Seven and a half pounds across twelve guests is 10 ounces a head. A Thanksgiving plate holds 6 to 8 ounces of sliced turkey next to the potatoes and stuffing, so 10 covers the first pass and the person who comes back for a second piece of breast.

The neck and giblets sit inside that 15 pounds too, three-quarters of a pound to a pound of weight you paid for and did not serve.

## The Number Moves With the Eaters

| Guest or table | Raw pounds each | Meat on the plate |
|---|---|---|
| Adult, average appetite | 1.25 | 10 oz |
| Teenager or big eater | 1.5 | 12 oz |
| Child under 10 | 0.75 | 5 to 6 oz |
| Table that wants sandwiches Friday | 1.5 to 1.75 | 12 to 14 oz |
| Light eaters, no leftover plan | 1.0 | 8 oz |

Children are the line item people get wrong. The planner counts a child as 0.6 of an adult, so eight adults and four kids is 10.4 effective adults, which is 13 pounds of bird. You buy the 14 because stores skip 13.

Someone's uncle eats like two men. Add half a pound for him and put the savings in a second pan of stuffing, which disappears faster than extra turkey does.

Leftover estimates assume 8 ounces a plate. If your family plates 10 to 12 ounces, drop the headcount that bird serves by two and the leftover column goes to zero, which is the actual answer for why people buy a 20-pounder for twelve.

## Two Twelves Beat One Twenty-Four

Past about 20 pounds, two smaller birds are the better purchase. A 24-pounder runs 13 minutes a pound at 325°F, which is five hours of oven time before it rests. Two 12-pounders take 2 hours 40 minutes each and go in side by side on separate racks.

They also thaw faster. A 24-pound bird needs five days in the refrigerator at the 4-to-5-pounds-a-day pace in our [thaw guide](/blog/how-long-to-thaw-turkey-thanksgiving/). Two 12-pounders need three. That gap decides whether a fresh bird is still an option.

Two birds also mean more skin, so more crisp bits per guest, and each one fits a standard pan. The cost is a second pan and a staggered start if the oven holds only one at a time.

## Whole Bird, Bone-In Breast, or Boneless Roast

Boneless turkey breast roasts differently because there is no carcass in the way. It loses moisture instead of weight to bones, so the buy number drops.

| Cut | Yield after roasting | Buy per adult | Roast at 325°F |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole bone-in turkey | about 50% | 1.25 lb | 13 min per lb |
| Bone-in turkey breast, 5 to 7 lb | about 55% | 1 lb | 20 min per lb |
| Boneless breast roast | about 70% | 0.75 lb | 25 to 30 min per lb, to 165°F |

A table of eight that wants white meat only needs a 5-pound bone-in breast or a 4-pound boneless roast. What you give up is gravy depth, since drippings come from skin and bone, and dark meat for the people who ask for a leg.

Half turkey and half ham works the same way. The planner cuts the turkey to 0.7 pounds per adult when a ham shares the table, since the ham carries the rest of the protein.

## What the Meat Counter Actually Has

Supermarket birds run 10 to 24 pounds, usually in 2-pound steps, and the 14 to 16 range sells out first. Frozen commodity birds show up at 99 cents to $1.99 a pound. Free-range and heritage birds run $3.99 to $6.99.

Two label details change the math. Self-basting birds carry a solution of up to 3% of their weight, which on a 15-pounder is about 7 ounces of salt water you paid turkey prices for. And the giblets count toward net weight, so a 15-pound bird is closer to 14 pounds of roasting turkey.

If the store has 12-pounders and 18-pounders the week you shop and you need 15, buy the 18. Twenty percent more bird is roughly 40 extra minutes in the oven, a slightly wider pan, and two extra sandwiches. Driving to a second store is the worse trade.

## Cost Per Guest, Roughly

At $1.49 a pound a 15-pound bird costs $22 and feeds twelve for $1.86 a head. At $3.99 the same bird is $60, or $5 a guest. A 20-pounder at $1.29 covers twelve on Thursday and three more days of sandwiches for $26.

The bird is rarely the expensive part. Sides, butter, cream, and pies usually run two to three times the turkey line, which is what the [recipe cost calculator](/recipe-cost-calculator/) is for.

## The Order to Do This In

- **Pounds first.** 1.25 per adult, 0.75 per child, 1.5 if Friday sandwiches are part of the plan.
- **Then bird count.** One bird up to 20 pounds, two birds past it.
- **Then thaw.** Four days in the fridge for a 15-pounder, five for anything over 20.
- **Then the clock.** Weight times 13 minutes at 325°F gets you in the oven, and the [cooking time calculator](/cooking-time-calculator/) adds the rest and the 30-minute rest to a serve time.
- **Then pans and racks.** Two birds need two racks and a side plan, which is a different problem than weight.

## FAQ

### How many pounds of turkey do I need for 10 people?

Twelve and a half pounds, so buy a 13- or 14-pounder. Ten adults at 1.25 pounds each is 12.5, and a 14-pound bird carves to about 7 pounds of meat, which is 11 ounces a person with enough left for a plate of leftovers.

### Is 1 pound per person enough turkey?

For a table of moderate eaters with no leftover plan, yes. Twelve pounds feeds twelve adults at about 8 ounces each and leaves nothing for Friday. Below 1 pound a head you are counting on a big stuffing pan and a second protein.

### How much turkey for 20 guests?

Twenty-five pounds. A 25-pounder is hard to find and harder to fit, so buy two 12- to 13-pound birds and roast them on separate racks. Add 20 minutes for the second bird's heat load and start them 15 minutes apart.
