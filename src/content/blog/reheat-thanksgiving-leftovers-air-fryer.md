## Five Minutes at 350°F, and the Skin Is Back

Friday's turkey goes into a preheated air fryer at 350°F for 5 minutes, one layer deep, and comes out at 165°F with skin that crackles when you cut it. The microwave gets there in 90 seconds and hands you meat with rubber edges and a puddle underneath.

The basket is the better tool for this job. It is also the tool that ruins stuffing if you leave it in four minutes too long.

## The Basket Rewrites the Time, Not the Temperature

Our [air fryer converter](/air-fryer-converter/) runs on the standard rule: drop the oven temperature 25°F and cut the time about 20 percent. That rule was written for raw food going in, where the inside still has to cook through.

Reheating is a different problem. A ¼-inch slice of cooked turkey only has to move its center from 38°F to 165°F, and it has almost no distance to travel to get there. A covered pan of sliced turkey in a 325°F oven takes 20 to 25 minutes. The same slices in the basket take 5. That is 20 percent of the time, not 80 percent.

Thickness drives the number, not weight.

| What you are reheating | Thickness | Temp | Time | Pull at |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sliced turkey breast | ¼ inch | 350°F | 4 to 5 min | 165°F |
| Pile of shredded dark meat | 2-inch mound | 350°F | 5 to 6 min | 165°F |
| Whole leg quarter | 2½ inches | 325°F | 8 to 9 min | 165°F |
| Turkey wing | 1 inch | 350°F | 5 min | 165°F |
| Turkey meatballs or croquettes | 1½ inches | 350°F | 5 min | 165°F |

Double the thickness and you roughly quadruple the time, because heat has to conduct inward from both faces and the middle is the last part to arrive. That is why a leg quarter at 2½ inches needs nearly twice the minutes of a slice at a quarter inch.

Preheat for the full 3 minutes first. A cold basket adds 2 minutes to everything below, and it dries the surface while it waits.

## What Else Fits in the Basket

Air is the whole method here, so anything that needs to sit in liquid stays out. Wet casseroles go into a foil boat and lose their volume to the walls.

| Dish | Temp | Time from 38°F | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stuffing, 1-inch layer | 325°F | 5 to 6 min | Foil boat, stir once at 3 min |
| Green bean casserole | 350°F | 6 min | Foil pan, crispy onions last 2 min |
| Rolls and biscuits | 320°F | 2 to 3 min | Single layer, they scorch at 350°F |
| Roasted Brussels sprouts or carrots | 350°F | 4 min | Best leftover in the basket |
| Mac and cheese | 340°F | 5 min | Splash of milk, stir at 3 min |
| Candied sweet potatoes, no marshmallow | 340°F | 5 min | Cubes only, sauce on after |
| Mashed potatoes | Do not | Use microwave | 3 min, stir at 90 seconds |
| Gravy | Do not | Stovetop | Bare simmer, 3 min, whisk |
| Sweet potato casserole with marshmallow | Do not | Use oven | Sugar scorches before the center warms |

Mashed potatoes are the one dish the air fryer genuinely loses. A 2-inch layer of mash has no structure, so moving air dries a skin across the top while the middle stays cold. Three minutes in the microwave with a splash of milk and a stir at 90 seconds gives you something closer to Thursday.

Gravy separates under fast dry heat. A saucepan on medium-low, 3 minutes to a bare simmer, whisking the whole time, brings it back together. Add stock a tablespoon at a time if it tightened in the fridge.

## The Foil Boat, and Why It Is Not a Covered Pan

A 5.8-quart basket measures about 8½ inches across, which is 72 square inches of floor. Two cups of stuffing spread to an inch deep covers 30 of them. Fold a sheet of heavy foil into a boat with 1-inch walls, set it in the basket, and leave the top open.

The open top is the point. A sealed foil packet traps steam, and trapped steam is what makes leftover stuffing taste like the inside of a refrigerator. An open boat lets moisture escape upward while the surface dries into a crust, which is the texture you actually want.

Three things to keep in mind with foil:

- Food has to cover the foil, or the loose edge lifts into the heating element.
- Never line the basket floor and leave it bare. Blocked airflow is how these units cook their own wiring.
- Parchment works for rolls and vegetables, not for a boat holding sauce.

## One Basket, Four Foods: A 15-Minute Plate

The basket holds one layer, so a full Friday plate is a sequence rather than a single load. Potatoes and gravy run on other heat while the basket works.

| Clock | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Preheat to 350°F, 3 minutes |
| 3:00 | Turkey slices in, single layer |
| 4:00 | Potatoes into the microwave, 3 minutes, stir at 90 seconds |
| 8:00 | Turkey out, tent loosely with foil, stuffing boat in at 325°F |
| 9:00 | Gravy on the stove, 3 minutes to a simmer |
| 13:00 | Stuffing out, rolls in at 320°F |
| 15:00 | Rolls out, plate assembled |

Fifteen minutes for a plate that tastes like Thursday. The microwave handles exactly one item in that schedule, and it is the one item it does well.

Do not stack the turkey to save a round. Two layers means the bottom slices steam while the top ones brown, and you get both problems on one plate.

## 165°F Is the Number That Hasn't Changed

Reheated leftovers need to hit 165°F throughout, the same figure the [food safety temperature guide](/food-safety-temperature-guide/) applies to casseroles and stuffing. An instant-read thermometer into the middle of the thickest piece is the only check that counts. Surface browning tells you nothing about the center of a leg quarter.

The clock on the fridge matters as much as the one on the fryer:

- **Turkey and sides:** 3 to 4 days, refrigerated within 2 hours of serving.
- **Gravy:** 1 to 2 days. It is the shortest-lived item on the table, and the one people keep longest.
- **Stuffing cooked inside the bird:** 3 to 4 days, same as the meat.
- **Reheating passes:** once. A second trip through the basket dries the meat past saving.

If the plan is a plate on Saturday and another on Monday, portion it out before the first reheat rather than warming the whole container twice.

## FAQ

### Can you reheat Thanksgiving leftovers in an air fryer?

Yes, and it is the best method for anything that should be crisp. Sliced turkey at 350°F for 4 to 5 minutes, stuffing in an open foil boat at 325°F for 5 to 6, rolls at 320°F for 2 to 3. Everything comes out at 165°F or above.

### How long does leftover turkey take to reheat in an air fryer?

Five minutes at 350°F for ¼-inch slices in a single layer. A whole leg quarter at 2½ inches thick needs 8 to 9 minutes at 325°F, because the heat has to reach the bone.

### Can you put foil in an air fryer?

Yes, in a basket-style fryer, as long as food covers the foil and the edges are folded down. Loose foil can lift into the heating element. Do not line the bare basket floor, since that blocks the airflow the whole method depends on.
