## The Group Chat Says Four, Not Ten

Four at the table changes almost every number. A 3.5-pound bone-in turkey breast, 2 pounds of potatoes, 3 cups of cooked stuffing, 8 rolls, 1⅓ cups of gravy. Every side halves from the ten-person plan you wrote last week.

The bird is the one item that doesn't split cleanly. Four people at 1.25 pounds raw each works out to 5 pounds, and whole turkeys start at 10.

So the shopping trip gets short. Half the stuffing, half the potatoes, half the gravy, one cut of turkey instead of a centerpiece you will still be eating on Monday.

## The Bird Is the One Item That Doesn't Halve

A whole bone-in turkey gives up roughly half its weight as carvable meat. Twelve and a half pounds serves ten at two-thirds of a pound each.

| Option | Raw weight | Carvable meat | Time at 325°F |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bone-in breast | 3.5 lb | about 2.3 lb | 70 to 80 min |
| Two boneless breasts | 4 lb | about 3.6 lb | 55 to 65 min |
| Whole turkey, for leftovers | 10 lb | about 5 lb | 2 hr 50 min |

The breast is the right buy for four. At 2.3 pounds of meat it puts about 9 ounces on each plate, generous for a holiday where three sides are competing for space.

A 10-pound bird also works, for a different reason: about 5 pounds of meat, or 20 four-ounce portions, so Thursday turns into a week of sandwiches.

Watch the clock on a small roast. A 3.5-pound breast needs 20 to 23 minutes a pound at 325°F, not the 13 a 20-pounder gets. Small cuts have more surface per pound and lose heat faster, which is the same reason a [10-pound whole bird runs closer to 17 minutes a pound](/blog/turkey-cooking-time-per-pound/). Pull at 165°F off the bone and rest it 15 minutes.

## Halve the Sides by Weight, Not by Eye

Eyeballing half a casserole is how a dinner for four ends up feeding eight. These are the numbers, sitting next to the ten-person plan they came from.

| Dish | For 10 | For 4 | What to buy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Turkey, raw | 12.5 lb | 5 lb | 3.5-lb bone-in breast |
| Potatoes | 5 lb | 2 lb | one 2-lb bag |
| Stuffing, cooked | 7½ cups | 3 cups | 4 cups dry cubes |
| Gravy | 3⅓ cups | 1⅓ cups | 2 cups stock |
| Rolls | 20 | 8 | one 8-count pan |
| Green bean casserole | 9x13 | 8x8 | half recipe |
| Pie | 2 | 1 | one 9-inch |

Two pounds of potatoes peels down to about 1.6 pounds, roughly 6½ ounces a person. Use 3 tablespoons of butter and 5 tablespoons of warm milk for that batch, because cold milk turns the mash gluey. The [per-person potato rule](/blog/pounds-potatoes-per-person-thanksgiving/) scales the same way.

Stuffing runs ¾ cup cooked per person, which is 1 cup of dry cubes before they hydrate. Four cups for four people. A 14-ounce bag holds about 10 cups, so you use a little less than half and seal the rest.

## The 8x8 Is Not Half of a 9x13

A 9x13 is 117 square inches. Half of it is 58.5. An 8x8 is 64, so a halved casserole baked in one comes out about 9 percent thinner and done a few minutes early.

Set the timer 3 to 5 minutes short the first time. The [pan converter](/baking-pan-converter/) handles the odd sizes, including the 11x7 that lands between an 8x8 and a 9x13.

Depth matters more with stuffing. Four cups of dry cubes hydrates to about 6 cups, which sits 1½ inches deep in an 8x8 and bakes in 30 to 35 minutes at 350°F, against 40 to 45 for a 9x13 at 2 inches. It still needs 165°F in the middle, the same rule as [stuffing in a bigger pan](/blog/stuffing-per-person-thanksgiving/).

## Fifty Minutes of Oven, Not Three Hours

A breast frees the oven in a way a whole bird never does. A 4:00 dinner for four, worked backward.

| Time | What happens | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1:45 pm | Oven to 325°F, breast out of the fridge | Salt it 24 hours ahead if you can |
| 2:00 pm | Breast into a 13x9 on a rack | Probe at the 60-minute mark |
| 2:20 pm | Stuffing into the 8x8, upper rack | Same 325°F, 40 minutes |
| 3:00 pm | Stuffing out, tented with foil | It holds 30 minutes without drying |
| 3:20 pm | Breast out at 165°F | 80 minutes for 3.5 pounds |
| 3:25 pm | Potatoes on to boil | 2 pounds, 1-inch chunks, 18 minutes |
| 3:30 pm | Oven to 425°F for the rolls | 8 rolls, 12 minutes |
| 3:45 pm | Mash and gravy on the stovetop | Gravy takes 6 minutes flat |
| 4:00 pm | Carve and sit down | The breast rested 40 minutes |

Forty minutes of rest is longer than a whole turkey gets. A breast tolerates it because there is less mass to cool. Tent it loosely, since foil pressed against the skin steams the crackling off.

## What Not to Shrink

Gravy is the first thing guests ask for twice, so 1⅓ cups is the floor. Two cups of stock with 3 tablespoons each of butter and flour holds up to a second pour, and the roux needs two full minutes before the stock goes in or the flour tastes raw at the table. The [headcount math](/blog/how-much-gravy-per-person-thanksgiving/) starts from a third of a cup.

One pie covers four. A 9-inch pie cuts into 8 slices, and four people at a slice and a half put away six. Don't halve a pie recipe into a 6-inch plate: 28 square inches against 63 means the filling sits an inch deeper and the center never sets.

Rolls are the other trap. An 8-count pan browns in 12 minutes at 425°F, and a halved dough recipe still needs the same two rises. Make the full batch and freeze half the shaped rolls on a sheet pan. They bake from frozen in 18 minutes at 375°F on a night when you want four.

And do not roast two small birds instead of one breast. Two 4-pound turkeys take 2 hours, fill the oven, and carve into more bone than meat.

## When the Count Goes the Other Way

If four becomes twelve before Thursday, the arithmetic runs backward and the clock is the real problem. Twelve people means a 15-pound bird, which needs four days in the fridge at 24 hours per 4 pounds. On Tuesday, that window is gone.

Cold water is the rescue: 30 minutes a pound, so 7 hours 30 minutes for 15 pounds, re-bagged with fresh ice water every half hour. Tap water alone won't do it. The bird has to stay under 40°F the whole time.

Then 6 pounds of potatoes, 9 cups of dry cubes in a 9x13, 24 rolls, 4 cups of gravy. The [holiday meal planner](/holiday-meal-planner/) rebuilds that list and the thaw schedule from the new headcount in one pass.

## FAQ

### How much turkey do I need for 4 people at Thanksgiving?

Five pounds raw, which in practice means a 3.5-pound bone-in breast or two 2-pound boneless breasts. That lands about 9 ounces of meat a person after carving. A 10-pound whole bird also feeds four, with about 5 pounds of leftovers behind it.

### Can I just halve a 9x13 casserole recipe for four people?

Yes, in an 8x8. It is 64 square inches against the 58.5 that half of a 9x13 would be, so the layer comes out thinner and bakes 3 to 5 minutes faster. Check it early the first time, then write the time on the recipe card.

### What can I make the night before for a small Thanksgiving?

Stuffing, gravy, mashed potatoes, and pie all hold overnight at 40°F. Cool each one uncovered for 30 minutes, then reheat at 325°F for 20 minutes on Thursday. Cook the breast fresh. Eighty minutes beats reheated slices that dry out by the time gravy reaches them.
