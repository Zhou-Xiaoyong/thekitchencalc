## Four Days, and It Goes in on Sunday

A 15-pound turkey takes four days to thaw in a refrigerator. Thanksgiving is Thursday, so the bird moves from freezer to fridge on Sunday, not Monday morning when you remember it.

Most charts print three. The extra day is the difference between a bird that roasts through in 195 minutes and one with ice still at the thigh joint.

## Where Four Comes From

The USDA rate is 24 hours per 4 to 5 pounds, in a refrigerator at 40°F or below. That range is the problem, because its two ends disagree about the same bird.

Fifteen pounds divided by 4 is 3.75 days. Divided by 5 it is 3. Our [holiday meal planner](/holiday-meal-planner/) uses the divided-by-five version, which is fine for a small bird in a cold fridge and optimistic for anything else. Plan on four and you buy a day of slack for nothing.

| Bird | Fridge days at ÷4 | Fridge days at ÷5 | Into the fridge, for a Thursday |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 lb | 3 | 2 | Monday |
| 12 lb | 3 | 3 | Monday |
| 14 lb | 4 | 3 | Sunday |
| 15 lb | 4 | 3 | Sunday |
| 18 lb | 5 | 4 | Saturday |
| 20 lb | 5 | 4 | Saturday |
| 24 lb | 6 | 5 | Friday |

Round up, never down. Half a day of thaw is not something you borrow back.

## The Fridge Itself Changes the Answer

Thawing is heat moving from air into the bird, and a home refrigerator holds anywhere from 33°F to 41°F depending on the dial and how often the door opens.

At 41°F the bird follows the chart. At 34°F, where a lot of people keep a fridge they think of as "cold," give it another day past 16 pounds. A fridge thermometer on the middle shelf settles it for eight dollars.

Two things slow a thaw further.

- **Big birds thaw slower per pound.** A 24-pounder has less surface area for its mass than a 12-pounder, so six days is a floor and seven is safer.
- **The cavity is the slowest part.** A frozen bag of giblets in the middle of it is an ice block inside an ice block. Reach in on day two, when your hand fits, and pull the neck and giblets out.

## The Counter Is Not a Thawing Method

A turkey on the counter spends two days with its skin between 45°F and 65°F, which is the 40°F to 140°F danger zone. The [food safety temperature guide](/food-safety-temperature-guide/) keeps the whole zone in one place.

Bacteria need time and warmth. Counter-thawing hands them both. Fridge thawing is slow for exactly the reason it is safe.

## If You Are Late: Cold Water, 30 Minutes a Pound

Tuesday night, and the bird is a rock.

The cold-water method runs 30 minutes per pound, with the water changed every 30 minutes. Submerge the bird in its wrapper in a pot or cooler big enough to keep it under, and set a timer.

| Bird | Cold-water time | Water changes |
|---|---|---|
| 10 lb | 5 hours | 10 |
| 12 lb | 6 hours | 12 |
| 15 lb | 7.5 hours | 15 |
| 18 lb | 9 hours | 18 |
| 20 lb | 10 hours | 20 |
| 24 lb | 12 hours | 24 |

The changes are not optional. Standing water warms toward room temperature within the hour and stops pulling heat out of the bird, so an unchanged pot runs at half speed and drifts into the danger zone.

Fifteen water changes is why this is a rescue, not a plan. Overnight in a cooler with frozen bottles and a thermometer in the water works.

A microwave on defrost runs 6 minutes per pound, but home microwaves heat unevenly and the bird has to go straight into the oven. Parts and breasts work. A whole 20-pounder does not.

## What Thawed Actually Feels Like

A breast can feel soft while the thigh joint is still hard.

- **Press the thigh joint**, where the leg meets the body. It should give like the breast does.
- **Wiggle a leg.** Thawed, it moves in the socket. Frozen, it is stiff.
- **Check the cavity** with your hand. No ice, no frost crust, no cold draft.
- **Use an instant-read** in the thickest part of the thigh, off the bone. Below 30°F means ice is still in there.

## Give It a Pan With a Rim

A thawing 15-pound bird releases one to two cups of liquid over four days, onto whatever is under it.

Use a roasting pan with 2-inch sides. A 15-pounder measures about 16 by 10 inches, so an 18-by-13 half sheet leaves an inch on each side and no room for the purge. Bottom shelf regardless.

## If You Plan to Brine, Move Everything Up a Day

Salt will not move into ice, so a wet brine needs a fully thawed bird. That is the first line of our [turkey brine ratio guide](/blog/turkey-brine-salt-ratio/), and it means the soak stacks on top of the thaw.

Five percent brine, twelve hours, plus a day of air-drying for crisp skin. A 15-pound bird that would have gone in on Sunday now goes in on Saturday.

## Roast It Cold, and Add Time

A turkey straight from a 38°F fridge takes longer than a tempered one. Budget 13 minutes per pound at 325°F, which is 195 minutes for 15 pounds, then add 15 to 20 because the bird went in cold.

The [turkey cooking time guide](/blog/turkey-cooking-time-per-pound/) has the per-pound table for other weights and the one caveat that matters, which is that a stuffed bird is a different calculation entirely. Pull at 165°F in the thigh and rest 30 minutes.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to thaw a 20-pound turkey?

Five days at 24 hours per 4 pounds, so the Saturday before. Six is safer if your fridge runs near 34°F. Cold water takes 10 hours with the water changed every 30 minutes.

### Can I thaw a turkey in the fridge for too long?

Within reason, no. A fully thawed bird keeps one to two days before cooking, so the four-day thaw only works because the bird stays partly frozen for most of it.

### Can I refreeze a turkey that thawed in the fridge?

Yes. The USDA allows it, since the meat stayed below 40°F throughout. Quality drops, because the second set of ice crystals is larger and pierces cells, so the bird weeps more in the pan. Thawed in cold water or a microwave, it has to be cooked.
